My research combines economics, machine learning, text-as-data methods, and behavioral experimentation. The projects below represent the main active strands of my work.

## Supply Chain Risk and the Dual Margins of Firms' Labor Skills

**Authors:** Yongming Sun, Yiming Yuan, Hangyu Chen, and Xianhai Huang  
**Status:** Under external review at *Economic Research Journal* / <span lang="zh-Hans">《经济研究》</span> **[Chinese-language journal]**

This paper develops a dual-margin framework of labor-skill demand, distinguishing skill breadth from skill depth. It studies how rising supply-chain risk reshapes firms' human-capital allocation through product-scope concentration and supply-chain restructuring. Using Chinese listed-firm recruitment data and annual-report text from 2015 to 2023, the paper finds that supply-chain risk narrows firms' skill breadth while deepening skill intensity. The pattern is especially visible in reductions in non-occupational skills and increases in occupation-specific skills. The paper also examines heterogeneity between domestic and overseas supply-chain risks and studies how local settlement policy, supply-chain subsidies, and fair-competition review institutions moderate these effects.

## Three Decades of Female Inventors in Technological Innovation

**Authors:** Yongming Sun, Yiming Yuan, and Kang Zhou  
**Role:** First author

Using millions of Chinese patents over a 30-year period, this project documents the rise of female inventors in technological innovation. It establishes four facts: female inventors are slightly less productive than male inventors in quantity and quality, but collaborate more and have lower patent concentration; they are disproportionately active in technology fields related to human necessities and social welfare; the rise in female patenting is driven mainly by entry of new inventors; and senior female inventors mentor junior women through strong gender homophily, although the limited number of senior women constrains broader diffusion. Using China's 1999 higher-education expansion in a triple-difference design, the project identifies expanded educational access as a key driver of female inventorship.

## AI and the Reconstruction of Firm Task-Governance Boundaries

**Authors:** Yongming Sun, Zhiyuan Li, and Mengtao Chen  
**Status:** Revision after external review at *The Journal of World Economy* / <span lang="zh-Hans">《世界经济》</span> **[Chinese-language journal]**

This paper studies whether artificial intelligence shifts firm task governance inward or outward. It models AI as both a production technology and a governance technology: AI can increase internal execution efficiency while lowering cross-boundary coordination costs. Using Chinese A-share listed firms from 2012 to 2021, the paper finds that AI progress significantly reduces the share of labor-outsourcing expenditure, implying an inward movement of task-governance boundaries. Further evidence suggests selective re-internalization: firms increase internal high-skill human capital, outsource less total work, and retain more complex outsourced tasks.

## Task-specific Strategies Can Obscure the Inference from Observed Numeric Magnitude Judgments to Cognitive Processes

**Authors:** Yongming Sun, Sebastian Olschewski, Henrik Singmann, and Lukasz Walasek  
**Role:** First author

Numeric judgments are central to value-based decisions, but observed judgments may reflect task-specific heuristics rather than invariant mental-number representations. Across three experiments, participants estimate averages of two-digit numbers under different presentation formats and response types. The project manipulates set characteristics such as outliers and number positions to test strategies including focusing on extreme or recent numbers. A mixed-strategy model with heuristic weights better explains the data than any single heuristic alone, suggesting that numerical-cognition tasks can encourage context-specific strategies that mask stable cognitive processes.
